Research

Interaction, through two connected directions

I approach this question from two connected directions. In visual navigation and tracking, I study how a policy can adapt its behavior to the spatial context instead of following a fixed notion of safety, and how the dynamics of observer, target and environment can be modeled with explicit causal structure rather than as one entangled system. In human motion, I study how continuous movement aligns with language, and what a representation can learn from the temporal structure of movement without labels. In the long term, I want to make interaction an organizing principle for visual understanding.

DIRECTION I

Visual navigation and tracking

Learning Adaptive Safety Margins for Visual Navigation

Junyi Hu, Shuaihang Yuan, Geeta Chandra Raju Bethala, Anthony Tzes, Yi Fang

IROS 2026 First Author Agent ↔ Environment Interaction

How much clearance a robot should leave depends on where it is. A diffusion policy proposes candidate paths from RGB-D, and a learned critic scores them with a clearance budget that changes along each path. Map geometry supervises the critic only during training; the deployed system runs from RGB-D with no map. On HM3D it reaches 0.783 SR against 0.710 for NavDP, and the same policy, trained only in simulation, runs on a Unitree G1.

CST-WM: A Causally Structured World Model for Embodied Visual Tracking

Junyi Hu, Shuaihang Yuan, Jiazhao Liang, Yi Fang

Under Review First Author Causal Structure

In logged tracking data the robot's actions correlate with where the target is, so a generic world model learns to read target visibility off its own command. CST-WM splits the state into target-evidence, robot and observation branches and removes the direct edge from action to target evidence, so an action can change that evidence only by moving the robot. On a Unitree Go2 it succeeds in 20 of 30 real-world trials, against 14 for TrackVLA.

DIRECTION II

Human motion

VTaMo: Video–Text Alignment Model for Sign Language Translation

Junyi Hu, Zhewen He, Haomian Huang, Aoxiang Yang, Yi Fang

ECCV 2026 First Author Continuous ↔ Discrete Alignment

VTaMo achieves unprecedented alignment between video and text. It aligns the two in feature space with optimal transport: an entropy-regularized transport plan with a learnable null token assigns video frames to text tokens inside each sentence, and an Earth Mover's Distance objective over a learnable orthogonal transform calibrates the two embedding spaces. Because each word is assigned a contiguous stretch of frames, the alignment also segments a continuous motion stream into the spans that carry each word, without frame-level labels. Experiments are on four sign-language translation benchmarks.

SignDino: Self-Supervised Sign Language Representation Learning via Temporal-Axis Self-Distillation

Junyi Hu, Zhewen He, Haomian Huang, Zhenhua Li, Zhifei Li, Yi Fang

Preprint First Author Self-Supervised Learning

Image self-supervision distills between a global view and local crops of one image. SignDino applies the same objective along time: for a tracked body part, the local view is a shorter window of the same part, so the representation is learned from how the part moves, with no labels. Each part is its own stream, a small temporal Transformer over frozen DINOv3 features. Experiments are on sign-language video, where the hands and face carry the signal.

Real-Time Sign Language Translation by Prototype-Assisted Causal Streaming

Junyi Hu, Zhewen He, Haomian Huang, Zhenhua Li, Yi Fang

Under Review First Author Streaming Recognition

The first real-time translation pipeline built for the real world, and both fast and accurate: it turns a live stream of human motion into text. Online systems so far run a recognizer over sliding windows; this pipeline is designed for noisy live input. A confidence-aware pose representation discounts joints with low detector confidence, a part-specific tokenizer lets a short window retrieve the motion unit it belongs to, and the whole pipeline is strictly causal: it reads no future frames and commits each piece of text once. Experiments are on five sign-language translation benchmarks.

SignNet-1M: Large-Scale Multilingual Sign Language Video Dataset with Downstream Benchmarks

Zhewen He, Junyi Hu, Haomian Huang, Zhenhua Li, Yu-Shen Liu, Yi Fang

ECCV 2026 Co-Author ~1M Clips · 2,058 Hours

Video corpora of human motion are shot near-frontal, in studios, with few performers. SignNet-1M re-renders existing corpora with new viewpoints (3D Gaussian splatting), new backgrounds and performer identities (diffusion editing), and capture and compression artifacts, while the motion itself is preserved. Each clip is tagged with the factor it changes and by how much, so a failure can be traced to the factor that caused it. The dataset holds about one million clips (2,058 hours) built from seven sign-language corpora in ASL, DGS and CSL.

What’s next

Future Work

A human demonstration is normally consumed as a trajectory to imitate, which quietly assumes that the demonstrator’s body and the robot’s body are interchangeable. They are not, and that assumption is what makes cross-embodiment transfer brittle. I would rather read a demonstration as a specification of how the world should change — an effect stated over objects, relations, order and phase, deliberately free of whose arm performed it.

That reading turns my two directions into two halves of one system: getting motion out of video is how the specification is obtained, and a causally structured world model is what turns it into an execution. I am building toward a world-action model queried in context. At test time it is given the current scene and a human demonstration of a task for which it has no robot data; it infers the executions compatible with the effect that demonstration describes, and uses its own learned dynamics to check which of them would actually produce that effect — with no parameter update. The same effect admits many executions, so the model has to return a distribution over solutions rather than a single mapping, and the demonstration stays in context for the whole episode instead of being collapsed into a goal vector and discarded.

Two harder questions sit behind it. The causal factorization in CST-WM and the anatomical streams in SignDino were both mine to choose, which does not scale past problems I already understand — so can that structure be discovered rather than designed? And what evidence would show that a discovered structure is real rather than merely convenient? I expect the second question to be as hard as the first, and I would rather work on both than assume someone else will take the second.

About

Background

I am an undergraduate in mathematics at Fudan University, on a First-Class Undergraduate Scholarship, and a research assistant at New York University, working with Prof. Yi Fang. I am applying for PhD study beginning Fall 2027.

I also work on site at Liqing Intelligence, a world-model startup, on research and development for its embodied-intelligence stack, alongside market research across the field. A model there is judged by whether it survives a scene nobody curated, which is not the test a results table applies, and the two judgements disagree often enough to be worth watching. Separately I run an NSFC undergraduate project on automated grading of disc degeneration from MRI with Shanghai Ninth People’s Hospital.

My training is in mathematics, and it shows in what I reach for — optimal transport as a differentiable matching layer, factorizations chosen for what they forbid rather than what they permit — and in what I build myself: multi-GPU training, simulation environments, and the real-robot side of deployment on Unitree G1, xArm7 and Franka FR3. The gap between a method that works in a table and one that works on a robot is where most of the honest information is.
