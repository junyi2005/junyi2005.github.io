Research

Designing what a model is not allowed to learn

A large model will happily learn whatever correlation lowers the training loss. In embodied settings that is often precisely the wrong competence: a tracker that reads target visibility off its own control command, a navigator whose safety margin is a constant somebody picked, a translator left to silently guess a cross-modal permutation. Each fits the data. Each misrepresents the task, and standard benchmarks do not notice.

My work takes the other route. Wherever a task carries structure I can state — a causal edge that should not exist, a criterion that should depend on context, a correspondence that should be explicit, an axis along which a representation should factor — I put that structure into the architecture rather than hoping it emerges, and then build the measurement that would expose it if it failed to take hold. The constraint is the contribution; the diagnostic is what makes it a claim rather than a hope.

THREAD 01

Embodied navigation: how an agent's own motion shapes what it will be able to see

I started in navigation and kept arriving at the same realization — the value of an action is not what it does to the robot, but what it does to the evidence the robot will have next.

In CST-WM the tracker, the target and the environment form a small causal system, and a generic action-conditioned world model quietly corrupts it by writing the current action straight into the target-evidence state. Deleting that edge architecturally — and measuring that it is genuinely gone — is what turns imagined futures into something a planner can rank. In Adaptive Safety Margins the same instinct meets safety: a fixed clearance is wrong in both directions, so the margin becomes a learned function of context, supervised by privileged geometry offline and deployed from RGB-D alone.

THREAD 02

Pose as the interface between a person and the world: sign language

Sign language is the setting where human motion carries the entire weight of meaning, and it demands the same discipline — with the factorization given by anatomy instead of agency.

SignDino splits the signer into left hand, right hand and face, learns each stream's temporal dynamics independently and fuses only at the downstream head: the same move as CST-WM's branch factorization, transposed from a causal graph onto a body. VTaMo makes the frame↔token correspondence explicit through optimal transport instead of leaving a decoder to discover it. SignNet-1M names the factors of variation — viewpoint, scene, identity — so that a benchmark can separate a coverage blind spot from genuine training value.

What I want to work on next

CST-WM shows that one well-chosen causal constraint is worth more than a larger predictor. But that constraint was mine to design, and hand-designing them does not scale past problems I already understand. The question I would like to spend a PhD on is whether an agent can discover the causal structure of its own interaction — inferring, from acting in the world, which dependencies in its latent dynamics are real and which are shortcuts — together with the diagnostics that make such a claim falsifiable rather than merely plausible.

Embodied navigation & world models

CST-WM: A Causally Structured World Model for Embodied Visual Tracking

Junyi Hu, Shuaihang Yuan, Yi Fang

Preprint First author World models · Causal structure

A robot's action should change future target evidence only by moving the robot and changing what it will see next. A generic action-conditioned world model takes the shortcut and writes the action directly into the target-evidence state — plausible rollouts, wrong semantics for re-acquisition. CST-WM factorizes the latent into target-evidence, robot and observation branches and deletes that edge architecturally. The leakage Jacobian |∂H/∂a| falls from 0.112 to 0.001, and the model that cannot cheat plans better: +0.12 following rate and +0.15 re-acquisition under long occlusion over a matched world-model baseline that shares everything but the transition design.

Learning Adaptive Safety Margins for Visual Navigation

Junyi Hu*, Shuaihang Yuan*, Geeta Chandra Raju Bethala, Yi Fang, Anthony Tzes

IROS 2026 Co-first author* Real-robot

A fixed clearance margin is wrong in both directions: set it high and the robot detours in open space, set it low and it cuts corners in clutter. A diffusion policy proposes K = 16 candidate trajectories from egocentric RGB-D, and a learned critic carrying a trajectory-dependent clearance budget scores them. ESDF geometry is privileged information used only offline to supervise the teacher — the deployed selector runs from RGB-D alone, with no map building. 0.783 SR / 0.611 SPL on HM3D against NavDP's 0.710 / 0.529, and 8/10 on the hardest real-world scene with a Unitree G1 trained purely in simulation.

Sign language understanding

SignDino: Self-Supervised Sign Language Representation Learning via Temporal-Axis Self-Distillation

Junyi Hu, Zhewen He, Haomian Huang, Zhenghua Li, Zhifei Li, Yi Fang

Preprint First author Self-supervised learning

DINO's local–global game lives on the spatial axis of a single image. In sign video the meaningful counterpart of a local crop is not another view of the same frame but a shorter temporal view of the same tracked articulator. SignDino moves the student–teacher recipe onto the time axis: a frozen DINOv3 supplies per-frame embeddings once, and the only thing trained is a lightweight temporal Transformer per anatomical stream — left hand, right hand, face — about 30M parameters in total. Frozen features alone already beat the previous live-fine-tuned state of the art on How2Sign, full fine-tuning reaches 17.9 BLEU-4, and all 16 ASL-Lex phonological features improve.

VTaMo: Video–Text Alignment Model for Sign Language Translation

Junyi Hu, Zhewen He, Haomian Huang, Aoxiang Yang, Yi Fang

ECCV 2026 First author Gloss-free SLT

Gloss-free translation asks the decoder to learn translation and silently discover a latent cross-modal permutation at once — fragile, because sign languages do not follow spoken word order. VTaMo makes the alignment explicit at three granularities: entropy-regularized optimal transport with a learnable null token for local frame↔token correspondence, a learnable orthogonal transform over a memory queue for global calibration, and position-aligned contrastive learning over reordered features. A text-only recovery model restores fluent sentences from the decoded pseudo-gloss.

SignNet-1M: Large-Scale Multilingual Sign Language Video Dataset with Downstream Benchmarks

Zhewen He, Junyi Hu, Haomian Huang, Zhenhua Li, Yu-Shen Liu, Yi Fang

ECCV 2026 Co-author ~1M clips · 2,058 h

Existing SLT benchmarks are shot near-frontal, in studios, with a handful of signers, so state-of-the-art models break under viewpoint, background and signer shift. SignNet-1M is ~1M augmented clips across ASL, DGS and CSL from seven source corpora, varied along three structure-preserving axes — 3DGS novel-view rendering, diffusion-based scene editing, and cross-reenactment identity substitution — each tagged with its factor axis and severity. The paired Orig / Zero-shot / Trained protocol separates the coverage blind spot of existing benchmarks from the training value of augmented data; training on SignNet-1M recovers up to +14.71 BLEU-4.
