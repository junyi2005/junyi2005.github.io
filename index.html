Research

Making the correspondence explicit

Motion reaches a model as a continuous visual stream. What the model has to produce from it is discrete and structured: a word, a matching frame, a judgement about what will still be visible a second from now. Between those two lives a correspondence, and the prevailing habit is to leave it implicit — to hand a large decoder the raw stream and hope the alignment falls out of the loss.

I would rather state it. Each of my papers takes one stage of that bridge and makes it explicit enough to be built, inspected and falsified — and the stages compose into a single pipeline from raw video to structure.

01 · EXTRACT

Recovering a motion signal worth aligning

Nothing downstream survives a bad signal. SignNet-1M varies a million clips along viewpoint, scene and identity while preserving the structure that carries meaning, and its paired protocol separates a benchmark's coverage blind spot from the genuine training value of the added data. SignDino drops pose-driven cropping for a detector-and-tracker front end, so hands lost to motion blur and self-occlusion are recovered instead of silently written off as absent.

02 · REPRESENT

Choosing the axes a signal should factor along

A signer is not one blob. The left hand, the right hand and the face carry different information and move on different timescales, so SignDino gives each its own temporal encoder and fuses only at the downstream head — transposing DINO's local–global self-distillation off the spatial axis of an image and onto the time axis of one tracked articulator. CST-WM applies the same reasoning to an agent, separating what is target evidence from what is ego-motion.

03 · ALIGN

Mapping continuous motion onto discrete units

This is the stage I care about most. Gloss-free sign translation asks one decoder to learn translation and silently discover a cross-modal permutation at the same time; sign languages do not follow spoken word order, so it typically learns neither cleanly. VTaMo makes the correspondence a first-class object: entropy-regularized optimal transport gives an explicit frame↔token coupling, with a learnable null token for frames that should match nothing, and a learnable orthogonal transform calibrates the two embedding spaces globally before contrastive learning runs over the reordered features. A classical matching algorithm, made differentiable and put inside the network.

04 · INTERACT

When the observer moves, what may a prediction depend on?

An egocentric camera makes motion and observation mutually dependent, and a predictor will happily exploit the wrong half of that dependency. CST-WM writes the tracker, the target and the scene as one small explicit causal structure and blocks the dependency that is convenient and wrong — reading target evidence off the robot's own command — then measures the leakage Jacobian to show the constraint really holds. Adaptive Safety Margins refuses a different constant: how much clearance a path needs becomes a function of what the camera sees, learned against privileged geometry offline and deployed from RGB-D alone.

Where I want to take this

Correspondence is the part of this that should generalize. Flow, stereo and SLAM built correspondence between views of a mostly rigid world; what I keep running into is the harder case, where the two sides have genuinely different structure — a deforming human body against a discrete linguistic sequence, an action against the evidence it will produce. I want to work on general mechanisms for establishing correspondence across that kind of gap, and on evaluation sharp enough to tell a correspondence that is real from one that merely looks plausible.

VTaMo: Video–Text Alignment Model for Sign Language Translation

Junyi Hu, Zhewen He, Haomian Huang, Aoxiang Yang, Yi Fang

ECCV 2026 First author Video–text correspondence

Gloss-free translation asks the decoder to learn translation and silently discover a latent cross-modal permutation at once — fragile, because sign languages do not follow spoken word order. VTaMo makes the alignment explicit at three granularities: entropy-regularized optimal transport with a learnable null token for local frame↔token correspondence, a learnable orthogonal transform over a memory queue for global calibration, and position-aligned contrastive learning over reordered features. A text-only recovery model restores fluent sentences from the decoded pseudo-gloss.

SignNet-1M: Large-Scale Multilingual Sign Language Video Dataset with Downstream Benchmarks

Zhewen He, Junyi Hu, Haomian Huang, Zhenhua Li, Yu-Shen Liu, Yi Fang

ECCV 2026 Co-author ~1M clips · 2,058 h

Existing SLT benchmarks are shot near-frontal, in studios, with a handful of signers, so state-of-the-art models break under viewpoint, background and signer shift. SignNet-1M is ~1M augmented clips across ASL, DGS and CSL from seven source corpora, varied along three structure-preserving axes — 3DGS novel-view rendering, diffusion-based scene editing, and cross-reenactment identity substitution — each tagged with its factor axis and severity. The paired Orig / Zero-shot / Trained protocol separates the coverage blind spot of existing benchmarks from the training value of augmented data; training on SignNet-1M recovers up to +14.71 BLEU-4.

Learning Adaptive Safety Margins for Visual Navigation

Junyi Hu, Shuaihang Yuan, Geeta Chandra Raju Bethala, Anthony Tzes, Yi Fang

IROS 2026 First author Real-robot

A fixed clearance margin is wrong in both directions: set it high and the robot detours in open space, set it low and it cuts corners in clutter. A diffusion policy proposes K = 16 candidate trajectories from egocentric RGB-D, and a learned critic carrying a trajectory-dependent clearance budget scores them. ESDF geometry is privileged information used only offline to supervise the teacher — the deployed selector runs from RGB-D alone, with no map building. 0.783 SR / 0.611 SPL on HM3D against NavDP's 0.710 / 0.529, and 8/10 on the hardest real-world scene with a Unitree G1 trained purely in simulation.

SignDino: Self-Supervised Sign Language Representation Learning via Temporal-Axis Self-Distillation

Junyi Hu, Zhewen He, Haomian Huang, Zhenhua Li, Zhifei Li, Yi Fang

Preprint First author Self-supervised learning

DINO's local–global game lives on the spatial axis of a single image. In sign video the meaningful counterpart of a local crop is not another view of the same frame but a shorter temporal view of the same tracked articulator. SignDino moves the student–teacher recipe onto the time axis: a frozen DINOv3 supplies per-frame embeddings once, and the only thing trained is a lightweight temporal Transformer per anatomical stream — left hand, right hand, face — about 30M parameters in total. Frozen features alone already beat the previous live-fine-tuned state of the art on How2Sign, full fine-tuning reaches 17.9 BLEU-4, and all 16 ASL-Lex phonological features improve.

CST-WM: A Causally Structured World Model for Embodied Visual Tracking

Junyi Hu, Shuaihang Yuan, Yi Fang

Preprint First author Egocentric prediction

A camera that moves with the agent makes its own motion and its observations mutually dependent, and a generic action-conditioned predictor exploits the wrong half of that: it writes the current action directly into the state that represents whether the target is visible. The rollouts stay plausible and the semantics go wrong. CST-WM factorizes the latent into target-evidence, robot and observation branches and deletes that edge architecturally — the leakage Jacobian |∂H/∂a| falls from 0.112 to 0.001, and the model that cannot cheat predicts futures worth ranking: +0.12 following rate and +0.15 re-acquisition under long occlusion over a matched baseline that shares everything but the transition design.

Research statement

Motion is continuous; meaning is not

The gap

Motion reaches a model as a continuous stream of pixels. What we need out of it is discrete and structured — a word, a matching frame, a judgement about what will still be visible a second from now. Between the two sits a correspondence, and the prevailing practice is to leave it implicit: hand a large decoder the raw stream and let the alignment fall out of the training loss.

That holds up until the two sides have genuinely different structure. Sign languages do not follow spoken word order, so a decoder asked to learn translation and silently discover a cross-modal permutation usually learns neither cleanly. An egocentric camera makes an agent's own motion and its observations mutually dependent, so a predictor trained on that data will read target visibility off its own control command — futures that look plausible and mean the wrong thing. In both cases the model fits the data and misrepresents the task, and the standard benchmark does not notice.

What building these systems has taught me

Explicit beats latent whenever the structure can be stated. In VTaMo the frame↔token correspondence stops being something the decoder has to infer and becomes an object in the model: entropy-regularized optimal transport produces an explicit coupling, with a learnable null token for frames that should match nothing. The alignment can then be inspected, constrained and ablated separately from the translation.

The right factorization is usually given by the domain, not discovered. A signer is not one blob and an agent is not one state. SignDino encodes the left hand, the right hand and the face independently over time and fuses only downstream; CST-WM splits target evidence from ego-motion from observation. In both cases the axes were available for free in the problem, and using them cost nothing but bought a representation whose parts mean something.

The dependency you want is rarely the correlation you get. This is the sharpest lesson. In CST-WM the model can predict target visibility very well by cheating — reading it off the action — and no accuracy metric distinguishes that from understanding. Deleting the edge architecturally and then measuring that the leakage Jacobian fell to zero was the only way to know which model I had.

These are mechanisms, not applications

Sign language is where most of this gets tested, and it would be easy to read the work as an application area. That is not how it is built. SignDino is a modification of DINOv2/v3 — the self-distillation game moved off the spatial axis of an image and onto the time axis of a tracked articulator — and what it learns is pose-pattern structure without labels. The strongest evidence for that is not the translation score but the phonological probe, where all sixteen sub-lexical features improve: it has learned how articulators are configured and how they move, not how to translate. Likewise the core of VTaMo is a general matching layer between two sequences of different length and ordering, which we have since applied to temporal sub-task segmentation of long-horizon robot-manipulation video.

Sign language is an unusually demanding testbed for motion-to-structure correspondence — dense, fast, semantically loaded, and badly served by existing benchmarks. But it is the evaluation, not the design target.

How I try to work

A constraint that cannot fail is not a claim. Where I impose structure I try to build the measurement that would expose it if it had not taken hold: the leakage Jacobian and action-swap intervention in CST-WM, the controlled robot-motion-only check that separates selective insensitivity from plain numbness, the paired Orig / Zero-shot / Trained protocol in SignNet-1M that keeps a benchmark's coverage blind spot from being mistaken for the training value of data. Baselines matter for the same reason: the comparison I trust most in CST-WM is against a world model that shares the observation interface, planning horizon, candidate budget and action bounds, and differs only in the transition design.

Where I want to go

Correspondence is the part of this that should generalize. Optical flow, stereo and SLAM built correspondence between views of a largely rigid world, and the machinery that came out of that work outlived the tasks it was written for. The cases I keep running into are the ones where the two sides are structurally unlike each other — a deforming human body against a discrete linguistic sequence, an action against the evidence it will later produce — and there is no comparable general machinery yet.

Two directions follow. The first is correspondence that is learned but inspectable: mechanisms with the generality of an attention map and the auditability of an explicit coupling, so that what the model matched can be examined rather than inferred from downstream accuracy. The second is whether the structure I currently hand-design can be discovered — the causal factorization in CST-WM and the anatomical streams in SignDino were both mine to choose, which does not scale beyond problems I already understand — and, inseparably, what evidence would establish that a discovered structure is real rather than convenient. I expect the evaluation half of that question to be as hard as the modelling half, and I would rather work on both than assume the first will be handled by someone else.

About

Background

I am an undergraduate in mathematics at Fudan University and a research assistant at New York University, working with Prof. Yi Fang. I am applying for PhD study beginning Fall 2027.

The topics I have worked on were shaped in large part by the groups I joined. What I take to be my own is the judgement I formed while doing them:

  • A mechanism described as belonging to an application is usually undersold. The honest test is to run it somewhere it was not designed for, and I would rather do that than add another point to a leaderboard I already lead.
  • A result I cannot imagine falsifying is not yet a result. The diagnostic that could have caught me belongs in the paper, not in an appendix — and more than once it has changed what I was entitled to claim.
  • When the structure of a problem can be stated, stating it beats hoping a larger model recovers it from correlation. Scale is not a substitute for knowing what your representation is supposed to mean.

My training is in mathematics, and it shows in the tools I reach for: optimal transport as a differentiable matching layer, factorizations chosen for what they forbid rather than what they permit. It also shows in what I find unsatisfying — a number that improved without an account of why.
